_Why it matters:_ Stephen A. Smith reveals that players themselves hold him accountable for his journalistic rigor, suggesting that athletes value direct and honest critiques of their peers, even if it means being 'harder' on them.

**2. Stephen A. Smith, on player legacy, team dynamics**
[03:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kpUBiZ3FpOw&t=222s)

"But it's like, they will come at me and I'm sitting there like, it ain't just Hall of Famers, retirement players. It ain't just contemporaries that's in the league on other teams. Sometimes it's your own teammates. 'Yeah, your ass was a little bit soft today. You you could have went harder.' And I'm like, 'Really? That's what we doing? You would have did it to me!' True. Okay. No problem. You know, and that happened to me, that has happened to me throughout my career. So a lot of times it's like, you know, I get these interviews and people be like, you know, 'Players don't fuck with you.' I'm like, 'Really? Sure about that? So how have I been doing this all these years? You know what I'm saying? How many people you seen come on First Take? How many people see you you seen come on my radio show? How much information have I had throughout the years where y'all looking at, 'Where you get that shit from?' So it's just an accident, huh? No."

_Why it matters:_ Stephen A. Smith highlights the internal pressure from players, including teammates, to maintain his critical journalistic approach, underscoring the complex relationship between media and athletes in shaping public perception and accountability.

**3. Stephen A. Smith, on front office, coaching, team dynamics**
[04:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kpUBiZ3FpOw&t=284s)

"There's influencers and then there's journalist. I'm a journalist. So it's like, you can talk and you can give your opinions. Nothing but love for you. Go ahead and do your thing. But I'm the one on the phone with the commissioner. I'm the one on the phone with the executive VP. I'm the one on the phone with owners, president of basketball operations, GMs, coaches, scouts, player personnel directors, etc., agents, etc., etc. I'm the one doing all of that shit. So it's like, I'm not the only one, of course, but I'm just talking about me and other journalists in the industry. And when you look at me and you look at my resume, my resume spans 30 plus years. I represent entities. So there's more of a responsibility that comes with it. So stop looking at me like I'm y'all. I'm not y'all."

_Why it matters:_ Stephen A. Smith asserts his unique position as a journalist with direct access to top NBA decision-makers, distinguishing his reporting from mere commentary and highlighting the depth of his sources within the league's power structure.
