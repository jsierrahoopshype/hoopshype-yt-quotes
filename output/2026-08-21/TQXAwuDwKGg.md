**1. Patrick Peterson — "I'm tired of having to be the one to like lead you." — Patrick Peterson on the pressure of being the oldest sibling** [01:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TQXAwuDwKGg&t=115s)

**Brice Butler:** "With that, being the oldest of five, there's a lot of pressure just to make sure you're leading by example. Did you ever feel like it was just too much pressure? I have an older brother, he's the oldest of four, and he used to be annoyed by the fact that I had to be the one that y'all always had to look up to. Even though he hooped, he went and played in college, but it was like, I'm tired of having to be the one to like lead you. So you never felt any type of pressure when it came to that with your siblings at all?"

**Patrick Peterson:** "Never, man. Just because our grandparents used to always say, God put you in positions for a reason. They put people there because he knows that they can handle certain situations. And honestly, I took it as a badge of honor being the oldest. My parents had me young. There's been times where I'm at the house watching the kids, getting dinner ready, things like that at 10, 11 years old. So I believe I took it as a badge of honor because my last grandparent who is with me to this day, Arthur Johnson, he kind of raised me. That's my mom's dad, so he kind of raised me and some of the things he used to always tell me, watching how he treated his wife, watching how he conducted his business, watching how his mannerisms around when everything gets chaotic, he's always just chill, because at the end of the day, life is going to life. You just have to know how to maneuver through it."

**2. Patrick Peterson — "She was like, man, you're going to be something special, I don't know what it is, but don't waste it." — Patrick Peterson on his grandmother's influence and avoiding dangerous situations** [04:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TQXAwuDwKGg&t=241s)

**Brice Butler:** "When the success started happening, when I say success, I mean like Rivals.com, people noticing you, now I'm a five-star athlete, we go to camps, now they know who **Patrick Peterson** is, he's the guy. Going back home, obviously people in your community, there could be some haters or they could just be, I was in the wrong place at the wrong time. How did you, being the leader that you were in your family, just make sure you were not in those situations, around those people?"

**Patrick Peterson:** "I'm going to tell you this as well, man. My grandma used to always tell me this, I had an uncle, I never met him. His name was **Jeffrey**, and she used to always tell me, you look just like **Jeffrey**. He had a scholarship to go to Notre Dame, apparently he was super smart, I think he was going to be a doctor. But super smart, and he got shot, he was an innocent bystander at his senior prom dance. A senior prom dance, when everybody get together, do all that stuff, he was just an innocent bystander, somebody just came by and shot up the crowd and he got hit. So as my grandma used to always tell me, you're going to be something little Pat, don't waste it. She used to always tell me that, her name was **Margaret Peterson**, that's my dad's mom. She was like, man, you're going to be something special, I don't know what it is, but don't waste it. I'm getting goosebumps talking about it right now. She's like, just don't waste it. So when I always heard her voice in the back of my mind, my boys, you can ask my boys, **Bobcat** to this day, when we were in high school, they wanted to go out, 'P, let's roll out to the let out.' 'Nah, I'm cool, I'm straight.'"

**3. Patrick Peterson — "I was fooling around, didn't have the right GPA to play and I sat out that entire year." — Patrick Peterson on the academic lesson from his sophomore year** [05:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TQXAwuDwKGg&t=352s)

Patrick Peterson: "The biggest eye-opener for me was when I wasn't able to play my sophomore year in high school because I was fooling around, didn't have the right GPA to play and I sat out that entire year. My mom and my dad, they were very strict on me about, we want to see you have a 2.5 or higher. So I didn't have a 2.5 going into my sophomore year, they set me out the whole year. I literally did not play football my sophomore year. It literally humbled me, it grounded me, because my freshman year I felt like I was a man. I'm skipping school because my cousin Walt McFadden, he was a senior, so I'm on senior day with them as a freshman. I'm skipping class, I'm going to the sports store to get spats the day before the game, so I'm running around campus like I'm a senior. I'm not really worried about school for the most part. Once that happened, I was like, man, you really got to have the total package if you really want to be something great, meaning you got to handle your business on and off the field at all times. That literally opened my eyes."
