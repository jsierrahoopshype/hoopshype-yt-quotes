**3. Rick Bucher — "Brown is not as impactful on winning as he should be for someone who boasted that he was the best" — Analytical critique of Jaylen Brown's impact on winning** [03:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14SF2v0K9Rs&t=211s)

Rick Bucher: "The first explanation was that from an analytical standpoint, Brown is not as impactful on winning as he should be for someone who boasted that he was the best two-way player in the league. It's easy to see why someone looking at him strictly through an analytical prism would downgrade him. He led the league in two-point attempts and he lives primarily in the mid-range. More than 60% of his shots this past season were taken from three feet out and inside the three-point arc. White and Pritchard both shot a better percentage from that range and per 100 possessions, Brown was only a plus-one between his offensive and defensive ratings. That was the worst difference of any of the Celtics' rotation players. His playmaking and ball-handling look suspect through an analytical lens as well. Per 36 minutes, he was behind both White and Pritchard in delivering assists, but well ahead of them in committing turnovers. None of that, of course, takes into account that Brown undoubtedly was at the top of every opponent's scouting report and therefore faced the most attention when trying to score."

**4. Rick Bucher — "The accusation of thinking you're the smartest guy in the room isn't an attack on being intelligent, it's an accusation" — Jaylen Brown's intelligence and the perception of arrogance** [04:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14SF2v0K9Rs&t=295s)

Rick Bucher: "The next argument raised was that Jaylen's intelligence rubbed people the wrong way with a scout and an executive saying that he always thought he was the smartest guy in the room. Those looking to play a race card immediately jumped on that as evidence that the Boston Celtics, or whoever made that contention, is afraid of an intelligent Black man willing to ask questions and speak his mind. Which misses the point behind that observation. The accusation of thinking you're the smartest guy in the room isn't an attack on being intelligent, it's an accusation of being arrogant. I've met plenty of incredibly smart people. The ones I admire and look for any opportunity to avail myself of their knowledge are the ones who don't act like they're incredibly smart. In part, because the more you know, the more you realize how much you don't know. Intelligence should breed humility, not self-importance."

**5. Rick Bucher — "A franchise can't possibly compete for a title with two players signed to supermax contracts" — Why the Celtics were right to move on from Jaylen Brown** [13:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14SF2v0K9Rs&t=816s)

Rick Bucher: "Here is the real heart of why the Celtics were good with moving on from **Jaylen Brown** at all costs, because they couldn't afford to keep him. If anything has been made clear over the last three or four years, it's that a franchise can't possibly compete for a title with two players signed to supermax contracts, which is what the Celtics had in Tatum and Brown. Two supermaxes take up 70% of the salary cap. Trying to build out a championship roster in today's game with the remaining 30% simply isn't possible. If Brown had somehow carried the Celtics to the finals, maybe they would have reconsidered, believing that Tatum and Brown are just so good that they can carry a team of undrafted, low-wage talent. But that didn't happen. The Celtics got bounced by a 76ers team that was summarily swept by the Knicks in the next round."
