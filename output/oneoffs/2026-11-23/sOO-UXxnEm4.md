**3. Jimmer Fredette — "if you can shoot it from this cement slab that was far away that they can't block your shot" — Jimmer Fredette on developing his three-point shot as a child** [05:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sOO-UXxnEm4&t=312s)

Jimmer Fredette: "When I was playing in the backyard, we had this cement slab, mostly dirt, but then we had this cement slab that was pretty far away from the rim. When I was six, seven years younger than the players I was playing against, I would go in and I would try to shoot and they'd block my shot every single time. My brother was like, all right, I'm going to make this rule that if you can shoot it from this cement slab that was far away that they can't block your shot. So I was like, okay, well, I'm going to practice this shot. So I'd go home every day and I'd just heave it as far as I could from that spot until I started to make it. It was a three-pointer when I was like five years old."

**4. Jimmer Fredette — "what it did do is make me mentally prepared for anything" — Jimmer Fredette on playing against prisoners as a young player** [06:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sOO-UXxnEm4&t=404s)

Jimmer Fredette: "I wouldn't necessarily say it was sharpening my game, but what it did do is make me mentally prepared for anything. My next-door neighbor, he used to run the rec portion of two different prisons in upstate New York. So he was like, for the inmates that are going to be on good behavior, he asked my dad and my brother, would you be interested in playing some ball and just bringing some guys up? And I was probably too young to be in there, so I had to have parental consent to actually go in. You'd walk straight through and then you'd get into the gym. There'd be no one there at first, but there'd be prison guards with their guns at each corner. And then all of a sudden they open the door and inmates would come in and they would watch on the bleachers. They'd start betting. At first, they were always betting against us, right? Because we don't look like we're great basketball players when we walk into the gym. And they start betting against us and they're cheering against us, cheering for their guys. And then all of a sudden we start playing and we start hitting some shots and all these different things and it completely changes. 180, they're betting for us now and they're cheering for us all the whole time."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sOO-UXxnEm4&t=404s

**5. Jimmer Fredette — "if you wanted to get seen, you needed to actually get in front of people" — Jimmer Fredette on his recruitment process to BYU** [16:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sOO-UXxnEm4&t=983s)

Jimmer Fredette: "It's crazy, it dates us, but there was no Instagram or Twitter or anything back then. So like, you couldn't just put out a mixtape and have people see it on Facebook and Instagram and all these things, right? So if you wanted to get seen, you needed to actually get in front of people. So if my dad was like, hey, this may be a good option, they have a new coach, **Dave Rose**, who runs a really great style of offense that may fit yourself, they're starting to play a little bit better. But I didn't know much about the basketball program because they used to play at like 10:00 or 11:00 at night East Coast time. So I wasn't up watching their games. It wasn't like I could watch Syracuse or North Carolina or UConn, right? You can see all of those games all the time. So I was like, all right, let's do it, let's go out. So I just went to a generic basketball camp. Most of the time, kids are going and being babysat, right? It's not an actual elite camp. So I'm like, let's go, I did it, obviously right from the jump, was playing really well, was getting noticed by the players that were helping and then a coach would come in and be like, all right, what's going on here? And he would ask me who I was and they were like, hey, do you have a father that's here? And I'd be like, yeah, I showed him my dad and then every single time they kept moving me up."
