_Speakers identified: Pierre Andresen, Dwight Howard, Brandon McCoy, Nick Young, Stan Verrett, Isaiah Austin, Patrick McCaw, Calvin Murphy, Chris Haynes, Jordan Crawford_

**1. Stan Verrett — "There needs to be somewhere where guys who are leaving the NBA still have a chance to play." — Ice Cube created the Big3 to give former NBA players a platform** [02:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=136s)

**Pierre Andresen:** "Back in the day, when it was done in the NBA, that's kind of all we saw. And now we kind of get this extended career for guys that we're not tired of watching. I still enjoy watching **Michael Beasley**, **Dwight Howard**, **Lance Stephenson**, **Mario Chalmers**, you know, guys who did big shots to win championships in the NBA, they still got it in them."

**Stan Verrett:** "It was a great idea for **Ice Cube**. He said he got the idea after watching Kobe's last game. He said, 'I'm never going to get to see Kobe play again.' He was like, 'There needs to be somewhere where guys who are leaving the NBA still have a chance to play.' And so that was what gave him the impetus to create the league."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=136s

**2. Dwight Howard — "My first couple years in the league I was the main guy, so basically I had my opportunity." — Dwight Howard on the freedom of playing in the Big3 versus the NBA** [03:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=228s)

**Pierre Andresen:** "How does that feel finally putting in probably more than 10,000 hours to have a little bit more freedom?"

**Dwight Howard:** "My first couple years in the league I was the main guy, so basically I had my opportunity to do whatever I want. And back then, the game wasn't for bigs to shoot. It wasn't for bigs to be doing anything, dribble ball handling, doing all that stuff. So I had to find a way to stay on the floor by doing what was asked. And back then it was dunks. And so as I got older, guys were like, 'Why are you outside here? Just go dunk on everybody.' So it's kind of like everybody was like, 'Hold up, why are you doing all this other stuff?' And I'm like, 'Because I can do it.' They're like, 'Well, why don't you just dunk?' You can, that's easier. So it's kind of a bittersweet for me because my old career everybody wants to see me dunk or do all that stuff. And I'm like, 'Man, I want to show y'all I got some guard skills.' But again, what made me dominant was being in the paint."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=228s

**3. Brandon McCoy — "They move the same way that they move when they're working out." — Brandon McCoy on the pace and physicality of the Big3** [07:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=456s)

**Pierre Andresen:** "What would you say is the biggest thing you picked up from playing with somebody like **Nick Young** or **Dwight Howard**?"

**Brandon McCoy:** "I think the biggest thing I picked up from them is just pace, you know? Every rep is like game pace, you know what I mean? As far as playing one-on-one with them, they move the same way that they move when they're working out. And, you know, just surprisingly how good of people they are, you know what I mean? They're like real down to earth, very like, you know what I mean, talking to them, want to be open and let you know, like whatever, whatever you want to know, they're going to be an open book about it."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=456s

**4. Isaiah Austin — "A lot of people think they're just going to come up in here and just walk straight off." — Isaiah Austin on the conditioning required for 3-on-3 basketball** [08:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=521s)

**Pierre Andresen:** "What you think is the biggest difference or the biggest adjustment you had to make playing in a Big3 3-on-3 basketball?"

**Isaiah Austin:** "Your conditioning, man. A lot of people think they're just going to come up in here and just walk, you know, straight off the couch and, oh, we're going to get 20. Nah, they like that. The physicality is so high, you know what I'm saying? And then on TV it may look like it's not fast-paced, but let somebody miss three, four, five shots in a row, you know what I'm saying, that possession just continues. This is, this is, this is the true share."

**5. Chris Haynes — "The NBA, there's drafts every year. They get younger every year." — Chris Haynes on the impact of NBA draft cycles on veteran players** [15:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=905s)

**Chris Haynes:** "He's been brittle with injuries this season, back injuries and something else. And so he's missed a few games right now. That's why the LA Riot are in the spot that they are right now where they're going to have to win out in order to get to the playoffs. But they're definitely going to need **Dwight Howard**, not just **Dwight Howard** to help you, **Dwight Howard** to make an impact with the LA Riot getting into the playoffs. They're a scary team."

**Pierre Andresen:** "It all feels like some of these guys are getting better."

**Chris Haynes:** "Yeah, no, it's a component. But think about it, it's like, it's not the fact that a guy like **Lance Stephenson**, **Dwight Howard**, **Montrezl Harrell**, Beasley, this list goes on and on. It's not that they can't play in the NBA, but you know the NBA, there's drafts every year. They get younger every year. And so when these teams draft, a lot of times they may draft two or three rookies, you know, on a team. Those teams have to invest in those young players, right? So when they be by investing, okay, they got guaranteed contracts. So at least three or four years, they're under contract and locked up, right? So that means a lot of the older guys that still can play are going to be pushed out. And it's unfortunate. So this is not, playing Big3 basketball is not an indication that your career is over with. It's the fact that sometimes, you know, you can be weeded out by younger guys and teams going in a different direction."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=905s

**6. Calvin Murphy — "I like the change of pace that the Big3 provides me." — Calvin Murphy on the physical, aggressive style of Big3 basketball** [16:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=995s)

**Pierre Andresen:** "But as a fan, what's the biggest thing you pick up on with the 3-on-3 versus the 5-on-5 in the NBA?"

**Calvin Murphy:** "3-on-3 obviously, you love the isolation game, you love the break the fire, you know, seeing guys go one-on-one. But what I like for me as a fan, I like the change of pace that the Big3 provides me. And what I mean by that, it's shit talking. You know what I mean? You know, almost throwing a fist to cuffs. I like, you know, physical ball. You're going back to the 80s style of NBA. And, you know, that's what the NBA has tried to shy away from over the years, of less physicality. But you get physicality here with the Big3. You get guys having a longer leash when it goes to arguing with opponents or even arguing with the officials. So I like just that heated, that heatedness. Like sometimes it needs to be toned down a little bit, but I like that aggressiveness. It's a street ball flavor mixed in with indoor basketball."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gm7OdZF8QtU&t=995s
