**1. Jordi Fernández — "I've had to learn how to be in this position. Probably basketball is the easiest part of it." — Jordi Fernández reflects on growth and learning as head coach** [00:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=31s)

Jordi Fernández: "Yeah, it's been growth, and I've had to learn how to be in this position. Obviously, when you get the job, you always believe that you're ready for it, and even though you've coached and you've been a head coach before, probably basketball is the easiest part of it. But then you have the head coaching duties and what it means, and the amount of people that you're responsible for, and the amount of people that you've got to touch every day. I think that's been a great journey so far. I've learned, I've grown, and the best thing is being around good people. This organization is built and is still building the right things for the right things, and we believe that we'll keep taking those positive steps."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=31s

**2. Jordi Fernández — "It's not about one person, it's about how we're all organized and how we work together." — Jordi Fernández on empowering assistant coaches and team collaboration** [01:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=77s)

**Sarah Kustok:** "You talk about that, and before we get into the big picture of the season, we had **Juwan Howard** on the podcast earlier, and in all of your coaches, I think through the course of the year we hear this, but he talks about how much ownership you give each of the assistant coaches and just the ability for all of you to work together. When you took this position, I know you've been a head coach at plenty of other stops, why was that so important to make sure that you felt like your assistants had that much responsibility in each of their jobs as well?"

**Jordi Fernández:** "I've learned that from especially **Mike Brown**, **Michael Malone**, and **Sergio Scariolo** is when somebody's investing in you, you almost work not just hard, but harder. Because you want to give back. It's the feeling that somebody believes in you and that you're responsible to do a job. It makes you have that willingness to do it right, do it for the team in the right reasons, because you want to grow and you want to take those steps, whatever your goals are in your career. But I think also is all of us, it's not about one person, it's about how we're all organized and how we work together for the team to be the best that the team can be. That's always the mindset that I've believed in, what I've learned from others, and the way I wanted to do it here."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=77s

**4. Jordi Fernández — "One thing that you can always control is your attitude." — Controlling attitude and fostering a positive team environment** [02:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=170s)

Jordi Fernández: "It's a non-negotiable. One of the coaches that I worked with, one of my mentors in player development, a retired high school coach, always said one thing that you can always control is your attitude. I know it's simple, maybe cliché, whatever you want to say, but if you show up with positive energy, and positive energy is contagious, so is negative energy. You can wake up every day alive and believe that it's going to be a good day and tell yourself today's going to be a good day. I'd rather do it this way than the other way. Our group has been outstanding, the coaches especially, because they bring that good energy to practice or anything that we do, and for the most part the players follow up, they respond the same way. It hasn't always been easy because the NBA is a tough business, there are wins and losses and everything else behind. But the environment has been very, very good."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=170s

**5. Jordi Fernández — "Wins and losses for you are not just on the standings, it's very pretty to say." — Jordi Fernández on controlling emotions and recognizing player development wins** [04:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=290s)

Jordi Fernández: "At probably controlling my emotions with the results. Because understanding that wins and losses for you are not just on the standings, it's very pretty to say, but to actually emotionally live with it day-to-day, it's not easy. You see the young guys get better. Our young guys played 6,400 minutes, the most that I think any rookies have ever played. There's different areas that they've got, that they're very good at, like **Yegor**'s shooting and **Nolan**'s playmaking, and **Ben**'s touching the paint, and **Danny**'s versatility, and **Draque**'s ability to guard the ball. All those things that we have the results and the numbers. Those are wins. They just don't show up on the standings. We all know it, we believe in it. We have a plan and the process that we're following. But for me personally, I had to be diligent with what we're trying to do, and don't get caught too much. Obviously, I think that being competitive is very important, and it's not just that if you win or lose, it's how you win and how you lose. I use this line, **Michael Malone** always said it, and losing the right way and winning the right way is very important."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=290s

**7. Jordi Fernández — "It's very collaborative here from the front office to medical performance, obviously nutrition, the player development on the court." — Brooklyn's collaborative player development process and summer commitment** [06:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=400s)

Jordi Fernández: "It's very collaborative here from the front office to medical performance, obviously nutrition, the player development on the court. Everybody brings in what the plan should look like, and then the players have to understand, and all this process works through the year. In season we do it in different ways because then we can show how their development impacts what they do on the court. Now in the summer, after they've taken their time, these guys have shown up here and ready to work. As you know, all this work is volunteer, and they already look older, they look bigger, they look like they've been working, their bodies look really good, and it's exciting because not just we have a lot of roster players here."

**8. Jordi Fernández — "This is literally the first summer for them to get better and start gaining." — Brooklyn's young players' first real NBA summer development** [06:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=400s)

Jordi Fernández: "The young guys have been here consistently, and this is the first summer of their NBA career. The last summer was non-existent. They were doing draft workouts, they got drafted, they went to Summer League, and we started right away, runs preseason. This is literally the first summer for them to get better and start gaining. Now you see the younger vets that we call them, like **Day'Ron** and **Noah** and **J-Will**, **Josh**, all these guys that are here, the same thing, willing to get better. That's what we want everybody to believe in: you put your work in, the summer is a great opportunity to take those steps, and now excited to see them when the time comes, in the runs and in preseason and see how much better we can be as a group."

**9. Jordi Fernández — "You acquire talent and you develop talent. Then there's the unknowns of the beauty of the NBA." — Jordi Fernández on balancing youth, talent acquisition, and long-term vision** [09:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=543s)

**Sarah Kustok:** "You talk about that. The team was so young. Even the veterans were players on other teams that would be considered young. When you look forward, I know there's moving parts and what things will look like, but to this next season, what excites you most about the potential, the possibilities, and just the direction moving forward?"

**Jordi Fernández:** "We may get younger, right? Because we're going to select most likely from one to three new players. But also at the same time, the ones that we've had, they're a year older. So it's very exciting because you acquire talent and you develop talent. Then there's the unknowns of the beauty of the NBA is free agency and all that stuff where, obviously, **Sean** and his group do a great job with, and there's always the communication day-to-day on how we want to take the next steps, through the different avenues, and but it's exciting because you can see all this work that we've put in, it's not just for the season or just for the games, it's always you have a long-term vision on how you want to get there and how you want to sustain success, which is the most important part for us."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=543s

**10. Jordi Fernández — "All that positive juice, that energy that we have on the gym is the music is a big part of it." — Jordi Fernández describes music's role in Nets practices and pre-game** [26:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fnp8VHnGwJo&t=1560s)

**Sarah Kustok:** "Who's in charge of the music at shootarounds or practices?"

**Jordi Fernández:** "So with the coaches, **Pope** is in charge of the music on the court. **Pope** is the most interesting man in the world. I don't know if you guys watch those commercials, but **Pope** can karaoke very well. Olmos as well as **Kelly**. And **Pope** is very, very versatile. I've seen him performing several times, and he's impressive. He does a great job. All that positive juice, that energy that we have on the gym is the music is a big part of it. Sometimes we bring our DJ here that he does a great job. Then pre-game, the coaches, we have like a little routine of like a coach is in charge of the playlist, and if we win the game, the same playlist is played the same way in the next game, and if not, it's a new playlist, and we rotate everybody on the coaching staff."
