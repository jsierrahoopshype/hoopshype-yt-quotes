**1. Uncle Phil — "we started this podcast just basically wanting to kind of glorify some of the stories of professional athletes" — The goal of the podcast is to share professional athletes' stories** [01:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FAKT6m4dZy0&t=96s)

Uncle Phil: "We started this podcast just basically wanting to kind of glorify some of the stories of professional athletes and their journeys and their families and the struggles and the sacrifices that goes with getting their kid, whether male or female, to that dream destination that they want to be in, whether it's NBA or NFL or MLB or soccer, whatever. Our whole point was trying to tell the stories because we've seen it, we've lived it, and we kind of understand that it is a sacrifice and everybody's story is different."

**2. Uncle Phil — "I knew that a trade was on the horizon for Nephew. So when that happened, focus shift." — The trade to Portland shifted the focus for the family** [02:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FAKT6m4dZy0&t=144s)

**Uncle Phil:** "Around January, I was telling a little bit about what I was privy to, I knew that a trade was on the horizon for Nephew. So when that happened, focus shift. I literally was when I was home, I wasn't even going to the local bar that I would go to watch football on Sundays or watch basketball games and stuff like that because again, in Memphis, it's not as big as a New York or whatever, so it's easy to be known for being Ja's uncle."

**T:** "Facts."

**Uncle Phil:** "So I didn't really want to get into a lot of the chatter. I didn't want to get into the injury talks. I didn't want to get into a lot of those type of conversations and stuff like that, and it's annoying as hell to me. So I really just isolated myself more than anything because internally we knew what was happening. So that's when we put a little pause on the podcast and stuff like that because when you're in front of these mics and these cameras and these phones in the world, not everything gets magnified, everything gets taken out of context."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FAKT6m4dZy0&t=144s

**5. Uncle Phil — "I was literally just filled up the rental and I'm 20 minutes from the house when I got the text" — The moment of receiving the news about the Portland trade** [08:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FAKT6m4dZy0&t=524s)

Uncle Phil: "I was literally just filled up the rental and I'm 20 minutes from the house when I got the text from Nephew that we're going to Portland. **6. Uncle Phil — "A lot of these young men that are fortunate to be in the NBA as a job" — Young players need support systems to handle the business side** [15:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FAKT6m4dZy0&t=902s)

Uncle Phil: "A lot of these young men that are fortunate to be in the NBA as a job, for a sport that they played as a kid, that their mom and dad might have played, or somebody in their family, it's like a lot of them are not ready for the full business realm of this. And the good thing is I think they're learning more of it in college now with the NILs, the schools that, you know, that business side of it, and you see it a lot more now where hopefully this prepares them for when they, if they're fortunate enough to make it to the professional level because I feel like, my opinion, college is professional sport now."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FAKT6m4dZy0&t=902s

**7. T — "I think what gave me a first look at it with reality was before, actually before" — Frank Martin's perspective on the transition to the professional level** [18:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FAKT6m4dZy0&t=1105s)

T: "I think what gave me a first look at it with reality was before, actually before, Ja made it to the league, we went and had a visit with **Frank Martin**. And he was just talking about the privileged kids, the rankings and all that. And he was like, 'Yeah, you probably was number one in the nation in high school, you probably was the best player on your team, but when you get selected and go to one of these Power Five, how many people you think were number one on their team? How many people you think was in the state, in the nation and all that?' And then he said, 'It flips when you go to the league and these grown men are trying to feed their family. They don't give a damn what you was ranking in high school or college or anything.'"
