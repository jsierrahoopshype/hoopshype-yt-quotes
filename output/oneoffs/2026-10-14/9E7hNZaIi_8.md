# 10 Questions with Chet Holmgren #thunderup — *Down to Dunk - OKC Thunder Podcast*

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9E7hNZaIi_8

_Speakers identified: Chet Holmgren, Andrew Schlecht_

**1. Chet Holmgren — "you have to release from the emotions of it good or bad, get back to baseline" — Chet Holmgren on processing the Western Conference Finals loss** [00:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9E7hNZaIi_8&t=53s)

**Andrew Schlecht:** "There's been a lot made about how you play in the Western Conference Finals. How have you processed that over the summer?"

**Chet Holmgren:** "I'd say approaching it the same way that I approached the summer before. Obviously, the outcome was different, the emotions of it are different, but you have to look at it objectively. You have to release from the emotions of it good or bad, get back to baseline and then you can look at the game objectively. Then you can look at what you need to take into the summer to work on."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9E7hNZaIi_8&t=53s

**2. Chet Holmgren — "you don't want to completely try to reinvent yourself, but you do want to try and build" — Chet Holmgren on building on the team's existing foundation** [01:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9E7hNZaIi_8&t=90s)

**Andrew Schlecht:** "Was there anything specific you feel like you worked on to improve just from that specific scenario?"

**Chet Holmgren:** "When you go into the summer, hopefully you have a foundation built and you don't want to tear down the foundation and start from scratch because then you're actually going backwards. You don't want to completely try to reinvent yourself, but you do want to try and build on top of that foundation and figure out what do I need to add on top of this to get to the next floor. You definitely put a different amount of weight into every scenario, but I try to take that and then continue on the foundation that I've already built. Continue to work on my body, continue to work on my shot, my handle, understanding different situations better, and it's no different."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9E7hNZaIi_8&t=90s

**3. Chet Holmgren — "I jumped Murray coming off the screen and he wasn't ready for it" — Chet Holmgren on his smartest basketball play against Denver** [03:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9E7hNZaIi_8&t=190s)

**Andrew Schlecht:** "What's the smartest basketball play you think you've ever made?"

**Chet Holmgren:** "In Denver, I think we're up one. Murray, Jokic pick and roll in the middle of the floor and they'd run it all game and they kept hitting Joker in the pocket and he's shooting these little eight-foot floaters and you know he makes those. So instead of just backing up and letting them have a pocket pass, trying to play two, I jumped Murray coming off the screen and he wasn't ready for it because I hadn't done it all game. I did it with like six seconds left and he ended up having to back up, take a couple dribbles to get back and Jokic saw that and he popped. But because Murray wasn't ready for it, it gave me the time to get back to Jokic. He ended up shooting a tough three at the end of the shot clock off the front rim."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9E7hNZaIi_8&t=190s
