# Dirk Nowitzki Watched Caron Butler Push His Own Kneecap Back Into Place — *ALL THE SMOKE*

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dNEHzuQfCPA

_Speakers identified: Dirk Nowitzki, Caron Butler, Matt Barnes, Stephen Jackson_

**1. Dirk Nowitzki — "I watched him push his kneecap down by himself, and he gets up and starts hopping." — Dirk Nowitzki recalls Caron Butler's injury during the 2011 championship run** [00:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dNEHzuQfCPA&t=4s)

Dirk Nowitzki: "I got hurt right around Christmas time, and then New Year's Day, I'll never forget, we were in Milwaukee, that's where Caron is from. I'm sitting out in a suit because I'm not playing, and Caron goes up for a rebound and just screams and lays on the ground. We're all rushing over there, and I see literally, he tore his patella tendon and his kneecap had dislocated, so it sat up top on his thigh. I'm trying to dry heave, it was nasty. Our trainer was like, 'We need a wheelchair.' That was one of the toughest things I've seen. Caron is pretty tough. He heard wheelchair, and he's from Wisconsin, his grandmother was there, his family. He's like, 'I'm not getting out of here in a wheelchair.' I watched him push his kneecap down by himself, and he gets up and starts hopping. You know how in the old Milwaukee arena, that was a drag to go back to the locker room. He walked that on his own power. I said this guy is built different."

**2. Caron Butler — "You can trap my body, you can't trap my mind." — Caron Butler on the mental challenge of watching his team win a championship** [01:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dNEHzuQfCPA&t=113s)

**Matt Barnes:** "When you see something like that from Dirk, obviously to me, I always stamp things on how your peers and your teammates speak on you and respect you. And to me, that's the ultimate respect. But the other part of the question was, how tough was it to have to sit there, you're one of the best three men in the game at that time, to have to sit there and watch your team win a championship?"

**Caron Butler:** "It was, so this is crazy. The penitentiary prepared me for this. I'm not, it's like you can trap my body, you can't trap my mind. So that was my process. I'm on the side and I'm just looking like, okay, I can be real selfish about this and just stay away, stay detached from the team, or I can just pour into this team more than I ever have. So I dove into that space. And I was just like, whatever y'all need from me, if I see some shit, if Rick can't say it, I'm going to say it to you. You're tripping on the coverage. Look at me, dog, I'm fucked up over here. I'll do anything to be out there. And it was just like a trickle-down effect from the energy that they felt like, damn, we can't let him down, dog. We got to do our coverages, we got to do our job."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dNEHzuQfCPA&t=113s

**3. Caron Butler — "I feel like I got a master's degree in basketball now because I was just in basketball school." — Caron Butler on his transition to coaching under Erik Spoelstra** [04:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dNEHzuQfCPA&t=265s)

Caron Butler: "When I first got over there, I was thinking about, okay, what is this going to look like? Because I got hired, obviously I was in the media space, I was doing all my shit, this globe-trotting. And Spo called me in the middle of COVID and said, 'Hey man, I've been listening to you on television, I love your messaging, I love what you're talking about. I think that you would have a huge impact in this space.' I looked at it like college. I'm going to a four-year university. I've been there six years. So I feel like I got a master's degree in basketball now because I was just in basketball school. Under the Godfather and under Spo, I learned so much. I learned how to apply information from a player development standpoint, for strategic planning, offensively, defensively, putting together game plans, being a head coach at the Summer League, being entrusted with some of these talents like **Jimmy Butler**, to Bam, to Udonis. Working on my craft, trial and error, and figuring shit out on the fly. I've learned so much, and now that I've had that experience, I'm ready to make that step forward."
