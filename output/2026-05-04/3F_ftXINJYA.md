**1. Draymond Green, on coaching**
[04:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3F_ftXINJYA&t=250s)

"As far as this statement goes, I think the emotion of a player and the emotion as a coach are two totally different things. And saying that, I've never coached. So I'm not shooting this down. I'm just saying the emotion of a player and the emotion of a coach are two totally different things. And what I do understand is that if I do want to coach, I can't have the same emotion as a coach that I have when I'm playing the game. But I also don't have that emotion in many other things that I do in life as I do when I'm playing the game. So I do understand that if I was to coach, that is something that I would have to be certain of. But I'm pretty certain that I could do that. I feel pretty confident I can do that. Needless to say, 1000% accurate on that."

_Why it matters:_ Draymond offers a rare, player-perspective insight into the emotional demands of coaching versus playing, suggesting a potential future path for himself while acknowledging the necessary personal growth.

**6. Draymond Green, on player legacy, team dynamics**
[15:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3F_ftXINJYA&t=914s)

"Steve Kerr's dad was killed when he was eight or nine years old by the Islamic Jihad when he was president of American University of Beirut. He said he used basketball to grieve the loss of his father. I can relate to that. Not that I lost my father, but so many things I've gone through in my life and I just go to the basketball court. You be going through something, you go to the basketball court at 3 o'clock in the morning because I can't sleep, but I'm going to go get some shots up. I can relate to how you grieve and you just pour it all into basketball. I think a lot of our journey is that. Look at Michael Beasley on Club Shay Shay. Your therapy is your sanctuary, the basketball gym."

_Why it matters:_ Draymond offers a poignant insight into the psychological role basketball plays for many athletes, serving as a sanctuary and coping mechanism for grief and personal struggles.

**7. Draymond Green, on coaching, contracts, team dynamics, player legacy**
[16:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3F_ftXINJYA&t=994s)

"Steve Kerr openly acknowledged that coaching tenures have natural expiration dates, and he might be nearing his. However, what's keeping him from walking away is his loyalty to Steph and myself. I can tell when he ain't feeling good."

_Why it matters:_ Draymond reveals Steve Kerr's deep loyalty to his star players as a primary reason for potentially extending his coaching tenure, offering a personal glimpse into the human side of NBA contracts and team building.

**11. Draymond Green, on front office, rumors, league policy**
[26:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3F_ftXINJYA&t=1599s)

"The NBA proposes a new lottery system to combat tanking. Lottery expands for 14 to 16 teams. There's a relegation zone: bottom three teams get fewer odds for the number one pick. Teams outside of the bottom three get better lottery odds. Play-in teams also receive lottery balls. No team can win number one pick in back-to-back years. No team can get three straight top-five picks. Picks 12 through 15 can no longer be protected. I think doing something to combat tanking is great for the NBA because ultimately there are 30 teams, and all 30 should be trying to win. What happened down the stretch of this season was pathetic for the NBA. As a player, it was pathetic to play against those teams. It was really a joke. NBA teams can also punish tanking by reducing odds or moving picks. The system is a trial run through 2029."

_Why it matters:_ Draymond strongly endorses the NBA's new anti-tanking proposals, calling the recent season's tanking 'pathetic' and highlighting the league's commitment to competitive integrity.

**12. Draymond Green, on front office, team dynamics, trades, league policy**
[27:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3F_ftXINJYA&t=1659s)

"How this affects people: no team can get three straight top-five picks. The San Antonio Spurs as we know them would not exist. They had Wemby as the number one pick, followed by Stefan Castle as a top-five pick. That takes them out, so they don't get Dillon Harper. I think this is a good thing. No team can win back-to-back number one picks. I think that is great. Play-in teams, you didn't make the playoffs, you should receive lottery balls. I agree with that. I do love the fact that if you're the bottom three teams, you get relegated. There should be an onus on trying to be better. Ultimately the goal is to win games. Not tank, not lose to lose. The goal should be to get better. If these front office people are as good as they say they are, you don't get the top two or three pick, you should still be able to draft well at six or seven. This will put onus on people to be as good as they say they are."

_Why it matters:_ Draymond illustrates the profound impact of the new anti-tanking rules on historical team-building strategies (e.g., the Spurs dynasty), arguing it forces front offices to prove their talent evaluation skills beyond top lottery picks.
