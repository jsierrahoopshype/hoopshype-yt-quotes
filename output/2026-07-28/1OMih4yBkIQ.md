**1. Josh Kroenke — "Going into that second apron is not necessarily something that we're scared of." — Josh Kroenke on the Nuggets' approach to the second apron** [00:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OMih4yBkIQ&t=57s)

Josh Kroenke: "Going into that second apron is not necessarily something that we're scared of. I think that there are rules around it that we needed to be very careful of with our injury history. The wrong person gets injured and very quickly you're into a scenario that I never want to have to contemplate, and that's trading number 15. We're very conscious of that pushing forward and providing the resources that we can when the moment arrives. But that second apron, is it a hard cap? I'm not 100% sure, but it's something that teams are obviously very aware of going forward."

**2. Bobby Marks — "They're at $68 million in luxury tax penalties." — Bobby Marks explains the financial consequences of the second apron** [01:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OMih4yBkIQ&t=102s)

**Malika Andrews:** "Can you lay out what the Nuggets exactly are facing right now that they are in that second apron?"

**Bobby Marks:** "Certainly there's a financial element. They're at $68 million in luxury tax penalties. Remember, they've only spent $51 million as a franchise in their history, and that's since 2002. That number would increase to $112 million if **Peyton Watson** signed that qualifying offer. The most important thing as far as being in the second apron is that if they wanted to entertain a sign-and-trade with **Peyton Watson**, they're not allowed to if the salary coming back leaves them over the second apron. This is an organization that has zero tradable firsts. If they remain in the second apron this season, that 2034 first now becomes frozen. They would not be able to move that in a deal."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OMih4yBkIQ&t=102s

**3. Brian Windhorst — "They have not been able to land an agreement on a deal." — Brian Windhorst on the Nuggets' restricted free agency situation with Peyton Watson** [02:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OMih4yBkIQ&t=168s)

Brian Windhorst: "That dreaded second apron is 100% impacting the Denver Nuggets and their conversations with restricted free agent **Peyton Watson** because they have not been able to land an agreement on a deal. That's why **Peyton Watson** and his reps have been seeking out potential sign-and-trade offer structures. The Hawks, the Bucks, the Clippers, they've all emerged as leading suitors for **Peyton Watson**. In a potential sign-and-trade, the Nuggets initially had set a price this offseason of similar to the **Walker Kessler** deal of two firsts and two swaps. Now that price, my understanding is, a really good high-level player as well as a first-round pick. We'll see between now and the deadline for any type of restricted deals, as we saw with **Jonathan Kuminga** last summer, was basically up until training camp. We have about a month and a half before that period starts to come around."

**4. Brian Windhorst — "You do not pay $70 million in tax under the Sam Walton way." — Brian Windhorst on the Kroenke family's financial discipline** [04:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1OMih4yBkIQ&t=296s)

Brian Windhorst: "The second hardest thing is to keep the championship window open. That's why we're seeing the Thunder trade off players, that's why we saw the Celtics take their team down, that's why we saw the Bucks fail apart. And let me just say the three letters: KSE. If you know, you know. That is Kroenke Sports and Entertainment. The Kroenke family is one of the most successful sports families in the history of the planet. They have won championships with all of their teams. And they do it by having incredible financial discipline. **Stan Kroenke**, his father-in-law was **Sam Walton** of Walmart. He was taught financial discipline by his father-in-law. It is the guiding principle. You do not pay $70 million in tax under the **Sam Walton** way. The league is waiting to see what the Nuggets do with their expenses here, because paying this money is not who they are. It's not who **Stan Kroenke**, it's not who **Josh Kroenke** is. When they cut salary, is it going to leave a bruise like it has with the Thunder, or is it going to leave a gaping wound? And if I was **Nikola Jokic**, that's what I'd be very closely watching."
