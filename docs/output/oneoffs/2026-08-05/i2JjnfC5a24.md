**1. Donnie Nelson — "My dad was trained under Red Auerbach, and Red Auerbach had fast-tempo, fast-paced, run-and-gun." — Donnie Nelson on his father's coaching philosophy and small ball** [05:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2JjnfC5a24&t=349s)

**Andre Voigt:** "When you look at the NBA today, your dad must be proud, right? Everybody is playing small ball, everything is trying to be creative."

**Donnie Nelson:** "It is interesting how it comes full circle. My dad was trained under **Red Auerbach**, and **Red Auerbach** had fast-tempo, fast-paced, run-and-gun. In his first year, my dad tried to do things conventionally, but he listened to some of the assistant coaches that wanted to do things conventionally. He did not know better because he was so young, and then it was not working. He was not being true to himself and what he felt, and he was also losing. So he was thrust into this position that, listen, if I am going to get fired, I might as well do it for what I believe in and not contradict myself. That is when he started to, when his back was against the wall and people were asking for his head, that is when he flipped and he did the things that he felt. His dream team was always five guys that are 6-7 or 6-8, and they can do everything, and they can shoot it, and they are skilled, and you can switch everything."

**3. Donnie Nelson — "Our game got really ugly for a period of time. And it was because people could put two hands and just basically wrestle you down." — Donnie Nelson on the impact of rule changes on small ball** [12:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2JjnfC5a24&t=765s)

**Andre Voigt:** "You touched on one point that is pretty interesting because it is always lost when people talk about small ball and all the games involved, they evolved, but the rule changes. I mean with hand-checking being more or less outlawed, was not that kind of, I would not say the birth of small ball, but did not this, is not this one integral point of playing small ball that you can now dribble without people just pushing you off the floor?"

**Donnie Nelson:** "Yes, there are two big things. Our game got really ugly for a period of time. And it was because people could put two hands and just basically wrestle you down to the ground. That is why **Michael Jordan** during the course of the regular season was exhausted and could not use his athleticism because people were grinding him down and doing what with thugs, he was getting mugged on the floor. Finally, someone woke up and smelled the coffee and they made some rule changes that I think are benefiting us to this day. So one was, yes, you could not just wrestle a guy down to the floor when he is trying to drive around you. And the second is, the three-point line has got shorter and shorter every year, in that in the old days there was a certain amount of guys that really shot the three, but now it is a prerequisite of you being an NBA player that you have got to at least be a threat from three."

You know what players make sense. Sometimes JJ is just too small, sometimes he has some physical defensive deficiencies or whatever and he does not play quite as much or might not play at all. And Rick might go with a bigger backcourt or something. So I think coming into this, everyone understands it is nothing personal, but we have got to try to win ball games. **5. Donnie Nelson — "You have some foreign big guys on your roster, you have American on your roster big guys. How much can you develop them when they are once in the NBA?" — Donnie Nelson on developing foreign and domestic players in the NBA** [16:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2JjnfC5a24&t=1006s)

**Andre Voigt:** "You touched on being guys like **Porzingis** now coming in, **Towns** coming in, guys who would be five guys 10-15 years ago handling the ball, shooting threes. You think that we are seeing now, we are reaping the benefits of a change of of, you know, educating ball players? Also here in the United States that they are more taught like they used to be in Europe that the tall players need to shoot, need to handle the ball?"

**Donnie Nelson:** "Absolutely. It was, I think that generally speaking, over the years, the expectation is that the European guys can really shoot it and the US guys are really athletic. Now, when you have European guys coming in and having success because again, a third more of anything is a huge advantage, and we as coaches recognize that, the US guys all of a sudden, they are like, wait a minute, if I am going to survive and make an NBA roster, I cannot just, I cannot, you know, the days of me just jumping over somebody or being faster than somebody, those are over. So the emphasis on our US kids to shoot the deep ball has gotten more and more competitive over time and as a result, I think it has changed the way that we coach from, you know, grade school to high school to to to the pros."
