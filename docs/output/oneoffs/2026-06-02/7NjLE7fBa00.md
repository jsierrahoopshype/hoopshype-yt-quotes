**1. Doug Christie — "Another coach could not coach this team the way he does." — Rick Adelman's unique coaching style and impact on the Kings** [00:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=44s)

Doug Christie: "It takes many parts to have success and many parts are vital. If it were not for **Rick Adelman**, we would not have success here. Another coach could not coach this team the way he does. He would not be fun-loving, he would not have the style of play. You cannot see one team in the NBA that runs our style of offense, not one."

**2. Doug Christie — "You have to put your players in a position that they can succeed." — Rick Adelman's philosophy on player empowerment and team success** [01:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=99s)

Doug Christie: "As a coach, there are a couple things you have to do. I think you have to put your players in a position that they can succeed and maybe more importantly, that they feel they can succeed. They think they can succeed in this system and then they are going to play for you. You get people to play for you all different kinds of ways."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=99s

**3. Doug Christie — "Sometimes coaching is about moving out the way." — Rick Adelman's coaching style of allowing players to be themselves** [05:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=332s)

Doug Christie: "Sometimes coaching is about moving out the way. It is not always about stamping it and staying. Sometimes the talent is something that you need to get out of the way of. I thought that one of the things that Rick was able to do was allow us to be ourselves and still coach us. And that is a delicate thing."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=332s

**4. Doug Christie — "He trusted us and let us do us individually, but never at the expense of the collective." — Rick Adelman's trust in his players and team culture** [08:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=519s)

Doug Christie: "That is the part where he trusted us and let us do us individually, but never at the expense of the collective. And that is a really fine line. There is a lot of trust there, there is a lot of communication there, but there also has to be a lot of respect there. Meaning that if you are going to allow us to move like this, we have to respect you."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=519s

**5. Doug Christie — "I start seeing how we were warned up and then all of a sudden I see why we do it this way." — The evolution of understanding Rick Adelman's offensive system** [13:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=815s)

Doug Christie: "When we hit the floor together and he showed the principles, we started learning and then I started seeing how we were warned up and then all of a sudden I see why we do it this way. Because it translates to the game and this is how we play and these are all the options out of it."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=815s

**6. Doug Christie — "The man was the same. He wasn't getting too high when we're rolling and he wasn't getting too low." — Rick Adelman's consistent demeanor regardless of team success** [15:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=911s)

Doug Christie: "The magic is the man was the same. He wasn't getting too high when we're rolling and he wasn't getting too low. And then in the game, it was the same. Like, okay, we're down, run our stuff. Like, do we need to adjust? Okay, we'll make this adjustment, that adjustment. He was the same."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=911s

**7. Doug Christie — "Rick Adelman is in my opinion, the godfather of that beautiful style of basketball." — Rick Adelman's lasting influence on basketball and coaching** [22:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7NjLE7fBa00&t=1366s)

Doug Christie: "**Rick Adelman** is in my opinion, the godfather of that beautiful style of basketball. When I go to places, they say, man, we used to stay up until 3:00 in the morning to watch you guys play. And I am talking like in Italy and these different places. You just never know how you were touching people because not only was the show great, it was for the NBA."
