**1. Mike D'Antoni — "playing was much easier because once you finished, you could play well, you lose, you played well" — Mike D'Antoni on the difficulty of coaching versus playing basketball** [00:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oFxwuYhLDI4&t=54s)

Mike D'Antoni: "Playing was much easier because once you finished, you could play well, you lose, you played well, okay, so be it. You go home and you go the next day. But as a coach, it's only winning, winning and losing and somebody on the team is not playing well, that's your responsibility. So there's always something going on with the coach, it's much more difficult. But at the same time, when you do win, it is really rewarding. So a little love-hate relationship."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oFxwuYhLDI4&t=54s

Mike D'Antoni: "I've never felt the excitement, the highs and the lows as a player, you feel highs and lows, but not like as a coach. A coach is way up there over the top or you're on the couch in a fetal position getting beat to death."

**3. Mike D'Antoni — "I was lucky I played point guard the whole time, so you have to be an extension" — Mike D'Antoni on his transition from player to head coach in Italy** [03:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oFxwuYhLDI4&t=183s)

Mike D'Antoni: "I was lucky I played point guard the whole time, so you have to be an extension of a coach anyway when you're playing that position. My father was a coach, my brother is a coach. I was extremely lucky to have the career that I did in Italy where I was able to start coaching as soon as I quit playing, they made me the head coach. I kind of learned a little bit by baptism, they threw me into the fire."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oFxwuYhLDI4&t=183s

**4. Mike D'Antoni — "a coach is probably a product of his past experiences, good, bad, you learn from it" — Mike D'Antoni on the collaborative nature of coaching success** [05:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oFxwuYhLDI4&t=334s)

Mike D'Antoni: "I think a coach is probably a product of his past experiences, good, bad, you learn from it, you move forward. There's just so many people, **Jerry Colangelo** and **Brian Colangelo**, they gave me the opportunity to coach Phoenix. I was in Benetton and Gherardo Benetton, one of the classiest, nicest guys that owned the team, and I just keep on going. Dan Peterson was my coach in Milan for eight years, and he took me, I was coming out of the NBA, not having a great career, and I've lost my confidence as a player. So he took me, built me back up and made me as good as I could be as a basketball player."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oFxwuYhLDI4&t=334s

Mike D'Antoni: "I wanted each player to have a career year and to get paid, because that's what ultimately they're there for. I want their families to be taken care of and they're taken care of.
