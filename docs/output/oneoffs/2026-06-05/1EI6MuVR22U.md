**3. Marc Stein — "Brunson has been talked about more just because the impact that he has made as a Knick" — Jalen Brunson's significant impact on the Knicks franchise** [04:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1EI6MuVR22U&t=266s)

Marc Stein: "I think Brunson has been talked about more just because the impact that he has made as a Knick. And look, it might also be a byproduct of where I live. You have to realize that on the same day in 1998, the Mavericks acquired the draft rights to **Dirk Nowitzki** and swung a trade for **Steve Nash**, two MVPs on the same day. They had a very checkered draft history over the next 20 years, and then 20 years later, 2018, in the same draft, they trade for the draft rights to Luka Doncic and then get **Jalen Brunson** at number 33, second round, only eight picks ahead of your guy Jokic. And the Mavs, out of those four guys, only one of them finished his career as a Dallas Maverick. So there's a lot of pain where I live because Brunson, it's not just remarkable what he does at his size, the surgeon he's become offensively, the way he finds every hole in defenses even when they look like they're not there. He's still getting better."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1EI6MuVR22U&t=266s

**4. Marc Stein — "the Mavs had so many chances to re-sign him. Both he and Dorian Finney-Smith were eligible for these four-year $55 million extensions" — The Mavericks' mistake in not re-signing Jalen Brunson** [08:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1EI6MuVR22U&t=522s)

Marc Stein: "There's so much consternation to this day because the Mavs had so many chances to re-sign him. Both he and **Dorian Finney-Smith** were eligible for these four-year $55 million extensions. And the Mavs held off because they thought they might need to trade one or both at the trade deadline. And that obviously was a huge mistake in itself. But as that season went on, by the trade deadline in 2022, it was apparent that **Jalen Brunson** was going to go to free agency and get a much larger deal than 55 over four. And at that point, the Mavs finally tried again to sign him, they finally offered to him, and the Brunson camp basically laughed at them and said, we'll take our chances in free agency now, you guys had your chance. But the Mavs still had a chance. And people forget this, that at that draft, the Knicks shed a ton of salary. So that's a week before free agency. From the moment the Knicks started shedding salary, everybody was fearful at this point that he's going to the Knicks now. The Knicks are going to offer him a hundred-ish million over four, and he wants to go to the Knicks. They hired his father to Tom Thibodeau's coaching staff. But the Mavs still had the option at that point of picking up the phone and saying, Jalen, we completely screwed this up. We are deeply sorry, we've disrespected you. We want to make good, we want to make this right. We're going to offer you now, we want to make this right. We're going to offer you 150 over five."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1EI6MuVR22U&t=522s

**7. Marc Stein — "the challenge the NBA has is finding something that is sufficiently significant to put David Stern's name on it" — The challenge of honoring David Stern with a trophy** [29:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1EI6MuVR22U&t=1798s)

Marc Stein: "The league has real trouble coming up with something to name in **David Stern**'s honor. **David Stern** was the commissioner of this league for 30 years, and so the challenge the NBA has is finding something that is sufficiently significant to put **David Stern**'s name on it because he is the most significant commissioner and most the longest serving commissioner in NBA history. So like, you can't name the NBA Cup after **David Stern**. That's not, that's not enough. It's not enough. It's funny because years and years ago, I remember Adam Silver just throwing out, what if we called it the Stern Cup? He was just spitballing. He wasn't like, it wasn't like a serious thing, but like, that's not a like, I think the league knows that's not enough. But the reason I'm bringing this up because now we're in the finals, what also amazes me is the Larry O'Bry— like, everybody calls it the **Larry O'Brien**. The **Larry O'Brien**. The LOB. Because **Larry O'Brien**'s commissionership was not anywhere nearly as impactful as **David Stern**. So I've always thought, we've seen trophies renamed in other sports, why can't they change the **Larry O'Brien** trophy to the **David Stern** trophy?"
