**5. Draymond Green — "Front-side action mean I may want to run an action over here to get that first domino." — Draymond Green explains front-side action in basketball** [06:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kO6w5za2y0c&t=377s)

Draymond Green: "Front-side action mean I may want to run an action over here to get that first domino to fall, and then I swing the ball to the other side of the floor and now we run another action on that backside. So actually, you start getting dual actions into your offense to make that first domino fall. Now when you make that first domino fall and you get a switch with Wembanyama on the front side and you swing it to the other side and now you get Cat going in some action on the other side, Wembanyama can't just sit back there and play center field because you've caused the first reaction, you caused the first switch. And now it just throws it off a bit. That's why you were seeing all those **Landry Shamet** swing threes, **OG Anunoby**, **Mikal Bridges**, swing, swing threes. That's how they were getting all those open threes."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kO6w5za2y0c&t=377s

**13. Draymond Green — "Referees, believe it or not, side with the more aggressive team." — Draymond Green on referee bias toward aggressive teams** [40:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kO6w5za2y0c&t=2405s)

Draymond Green: "Referees, believe it or not, side with the more aggressive team. That's a tale as old as time. And they're going to get a benefit of the doubt to the aggressor, not the person who's on their heels falling back. Because I can't tell whether you're on your heels falling back or he pushed you back. You know, and so the aggressor, if you're going forward, I can tell he fouled you. If you're doing this and you pass the ball and it's a little short, I can't tell if he hit your arm or you it was a little short. So the referees are always going to side with the aggressor."
