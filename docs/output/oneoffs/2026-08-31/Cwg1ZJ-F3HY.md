**1. Byron Scott — "I would have never ever thought that the Buss family would not own the team." — Former players discuss the potential sale of the Lakers** [00:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cwg1ZJ-F3HY&t=43s)

**Jay Wagers:** "Does it matter to you who owns the Los Angeles Lakers?"

**Trevor Ariza:** "To me, it's like you would have never -- I would have never ever thought that the Buss family would not own the team, you know?"

**Danny Green:** "I think it matters."

**Jay Wagers:** "You do?"

**Danny Green:** "Yeah, I think for a lot of guys now that aren't in an organization as much, it doesn't care as much as when they were playing. Of course, it mattered when you were playing. I'm still around the team, I still work with the team, I'm with Spectrum Sports, so I'm there. So, it definitely matters. You can feel a difference even in the media and the aura around the buildings, the organization."

**Byron Scott:** "Listen, it matters but it don't matter because we got no say so whatsoever. You know what I'm saying? But it matters because of the fact that when you play for the Lakers, like we have, and it's under the Buss family, that umbrella, you just never thought or never imagined it ever being owned by somebody else. So, I'm sure just like all three of us, the first sale to the Walters Group for 10 billion, we all were like, 'What?' They sold? And then my second thought was, 'Oh shit, 10 billion, okay, well I get it.' You know what I'm saying? But you still have that initial shock of them being sold because you just never fathomed the fact that the Buss family would not own the Lakers."

**2. Byron Scott — "He made us feel a very much of a sense of pride of wearing purple and gold." — Byron Scott on the unique culture created by Jerry Buss** [04:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cwg1ZJ-F3HY&t=252s)

**Jay Wagers:** "Byron, you experienced the organization when Dr. **Jerry Buss** was alive. So, what did **Jerry Buss** create culturally that made the Lakers different from any other NBA franchise?"

**Byron Scott:** "I think the main thing is he made us feel a very much of a sense of pride of wearing purple and gold, that it meant something different than being in any organization, that it was more of a family organization than most organizations that were either hobbies for some of the owners or that was their money maker. I think Jerry really wanted it to be more of a family-oriented organization and he made that point clear from day one. And he also hired the people to do their jobs. He hired **Jerry West** to be the general manager, make decisions. He didn't go behind his back or go in and say, 'No, no, we're not doing this, we're not doing that.' He hired these guys to do their job and he allowed them to do their jobs. He was just first of all, a very unique owner, still to me, one of the best owners in any sport that you can imagine. And he treated all of us the same. He treated every player the same. He treated Magic no different than he treated **West Matthews**. He would come in the locker room and he talked to everybody. He was one of the most humble owners that I've ever met."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cwg1ZJ-F3HY&t=252s

**3. Danny Green — "When you think of Russell Westbrook, you think of one of the most athletic players ever." — Danny Green reflects on Russell Westbrook's legacy and competitive nature** [07:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cwg1ZJ-F3HY&t=420s)

**Jay Wagers:** "**Russell Westbrook** has officially called it a career after 18 seasons. What is the first thing his basketball legacy means to you guys?"

**Danny Green:** "First of all, congratulations, hell of a career, man. 18 years, that is no small feat. We've had some battles early on. I don't think we liked each other much. He's just cut from that cloth. But as time went on, we respected each other. When you think of **Russell Westbrook**, you think of one of the most athletic players, not even point guards, ever played the game. He changed the movement of point guard of how it looked. He was a fearless competitor. He didn't win a championship, but he got to the finals, he's won on a lot of different stages. He was just one of those guys, he competed against others and even his teammates half the time for rebounds, for assists. He wanted to be the best. Even when **Kevin Durant** got MVP, he's like, 'I'm going to try to be MVP,' he ended up doing it."
