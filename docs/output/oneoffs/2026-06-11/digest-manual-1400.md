## Steve Kerr: The Mind of a Champion | Grey Matter with Declan Kelly — *Consello*

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk

_Speakers identified: Steve Kerr, Declan Kelly_

**1. Steve Kerr — "I went to visit Pete in Seattle before I started coaching the Warriors. He just explained to me that coaching is really about values." — Steve Kerr on learning the importance of values from Pete Carroll** [09:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=561s)

Steve Kerr: "I went to visit **Pete Carroll** in Seattle before I started coaching the Warriors. I had taken the job but I hadn't started yet. I was trying to pick the brains of really smart coaches who I admired. I had watched his teams for years. There was this beautiful competitive joy and that's what I wanted to achieve. I would watch his teams and I go, 'That's how I want my team to play.' I went and watched their practice for three days and he was so welcoming. He literally had me in the huddle. I'm on the sidelines, he's like, 'Steve, come out here.' **Russell Wilson** is the quarterback and he's like, 'Steve, get in there, get in the huddle.' So I'm literally with 11 other guys, they all have their helmets on and I'm just listening to the play. Pete was amazing. After three days he says, 'Come to my office, let's talk.' I go in and he goes, 'How are you going to coach your team?' I said, 'You mean like what offense are we going to run and defense?' He goes, 'No, no, no, that stuff doesn't matter.' I was like, 'Oh boy, I got a lot of work to do.' He just explained to me that coaching is really about values."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=561s

**2. Steve Kerr — "Phil Jackson had been a great mentor for me, teaching our whole team the value of mindfulness and meditation." — Steve Kerr on the importance of mindfulness and meditation in coaching** [12:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=740s)

Steve Kerr: "**Phil Jackson** had been a great mentor for me, teaching our whole team the value of mindfulness and meditation and breathing. He used to take timeouts in crucial situations and just literally lead us in a breathing session. He would just say, 'Everybody, just deep breath,' and we'd all take a deep breath and no other coach was doing that. So I was definitely pretending to be more calm than I was, but I do think that it is part of my personality, something I learned from my dad, to listen and to take everything in and at least to appear calm and approach things in a relatively calm manner."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=740s

**3. Steve Kerr — "I started 20 games out of 15 years. I was always a role player, a bench player." — Steve Kerr on his role as a bench player in the NBA** [19:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=1176s)

Steve Kerr: "I started 20 games out of 15 years. I think I played maybe a thousand games. I started 20 of them. I was always a role player, a bench player, and that was just my place. Now I did have to find different roles on different teams, but it was always as a support player. I was never good enough to be a star player, but I think that's kind of the beauty of basketball. It really does take a five-man unit working together. The five best players don't necessarily make the best team, and I love that."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=1176s

**4. Steve Kerr — "The number one thing you have to have on a winning team is a group of players who understand what the game is about." — Steve Kerr on the importance of high-character players for winning teams** [22:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=1362s)

Steve Kerr: "The number one thing you have to have on a winning team is a group of players who understand what the game is about, that basketball is not an individual sport, it's a team sport. And that may not sound profound, but it's much more difficult to make that come alive because players are paid based on individual statistics and numbers, and it's just kind of the way the business works. And so you have to put a group of players together who are really high-character players. We had an amazing GM named **Bob Myers** who put together the rosters, along with his group, his front office. So we had really talented groups, but we had high-character guys who understood this concept of the game that you had to sacrifice, you had to be part of a group, part of something bigger than yourself."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=1362s

**5. Steve Kerr — "You don't ask them to submerge their egos. You ask them to bring their egos." — Steve Kerr on managing egos in a team environment** [26:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=1592s)

Steve Kerr: "I remember asking Coach K, and he had a great comment. He said, 'You don't ask them to submerge their egos. You ask them to bring their egos, but you ask them to do so for one goal and that's to win.' And that was really the message. Now it gets harder from there because everyone on the first day says, 'Yeah, we're going to win the gold medal.' It gets a lot harder when you're only playing eight guys and you got four rock stars who maybe don't even play that night."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2B6NPghwqk&t=1592s
