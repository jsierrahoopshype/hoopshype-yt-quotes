# Nets Fest Convos: Coach Fernández, Julius Randle, Day'Ron Sharpe, Moe Wagner and Keon Ellis — *Brooklyn Nets*

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE

_Speakers identified: Jordi Fernández, Julius Randle, Day'Ron Sharpe, Kenyon Martin, Keon Ellis, Moe Wagner_

**1. Julius Randle — "we have a good mix of experience with youth and I think that's going to take us" — Julius Randle on the Nets' mix of experience and youth** [01:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=73s)

**Jordi Fernández:** "How do you feel about the squad and how we're looking forward?"

**Julius Randle:** "It feels great for many reasons. Acquiring a three-time All-Star is extremely helpful, but it sets the tone for the rest of the young guys. Bringing guys that have been there before, Julius, **Michael**, **Kian**, **Mo**, **Terence**, so we have a good mix of experience with youth and I think that's going to take us to take the next step."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=73s

**2. Julius Randle — "I just try to lead by example. I had the greatest example in a veteran and mentor in Kobe" — Julius Randle on his leadership style and learning from Kobe Bryant** [03:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=204s)

**Jordi Fernández:** "What is it that you're teaching the young guys as they're coming up? And at the same time, what are you being taught by those in front of you?"

**Julius Randle:** "I just try to lead by example. I had the greatest example in a veteran and mentor in **Kobe** growing up. I realize the impact. I look at **Yae**, I look at **Michael**, **Joshua**, **Noah**, all those young guys, **Drake**, and I remember being in their shoes and I understand the impact that being had on me as a young player and it kind of set me up for my career of how I approach every single day, how I approach a practice, how I approach a game, how I approach a workout."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=204s

**3. Day'Ron Sharpe — "The offensive rebounding thing is motor for one, but it's intelligence and knowing the flight of the ball" — Kenyon Martin on Day'Ron Sharpe's offensive rebounding and court intelligence** [13:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=826s)

**Jordi Fernández:** "**Kenyon**, knowing what you know about **Day'Ron**'s game, what part of his game do you wish you had when you played?"

**Kenyon Martin:** "The offensive rebounding thing is motor for one, but it's intelligence and knowing the flight of the ball and different things of that nature. You can't teach that, you can't coach that. We did a drill when I was in college called get opposite where it was offensive rebounding drill and we drilled it. It's not a natural thing for everyone to have, especially bigs, the flight of the ball and determine where it's going to land."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=826s

**4. Kenyon Martin — "Don't nobody want to have to deal with that possession after possession, quarter after quarter, game after game" — Kenyon Martin on the importance of relentless pressure in the paint** [15:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=903s)

**Jordi Fernández:** "What do you see in **Day'Ron**'s game that you wish you had?"

**Kenyon Martin:** "Don't nobody want to have to deal with that possession after possession, quarter after quarter, game after game. It's what we talked about earlier, right? Being relentless, each possession matters. Don't nobody want to deal with that possession after possession. You don't want to have to box him out. You can't. It's exhausting, and they're not going to do it. It's only going to get better the more experience he has, the more minutes he plays, the more meaningful games he plays."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=903s

**5. Kenyon Martin — "If you're not having self-critique and self-conversation on how to be better at whatever it is you're doing" — Kenyon Martin on the importance of self-critique for NBA players** [21:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=1272s)

**Jordi Fernández:** "What has been your foundation in your career?"

**Kenyon Martin:** "It's this thing that most of us have in our house. It's right by your sink. It's like a reflection thing. It's called a mirror. You look at yourself every day, which you should. And if you're not having self-critique and self-conversation on how to be better at whatever it is you're doing, it's just that we play sports for a living. **Muggsy Bogues** was 5'3", **Michael Adams** was 5'7", **Earl Boykins** was 5'5". There's guys that's done it. It's a little harder now because of the way the game has transitioned, but somebody probably told them the same thing: are you too small? It don't matter. Long as you go out and compete and you're going to fight, you're going to get some looks."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=1272s

**6. Keon Ellis — "the biggest question was not the skill and whether you can do it, it's more about realizing what type of player you want to be" — Keon Ellis on the process of finding his role as a young player** [26:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=1595s)

**Jordi Fernández:** "What is your perspective on the young guys that we have on the squad so far and how you feel like your leadership could impact them moving forward?"

**Keon Ellis:** "At the end of the day, I think it's not easy for young guys, especially if there's a lot of them, because the competition level is super high. And I think for me as a young player going through that process, the biggest question was not the skill and whether you can do it, it's more about realizing what type of player you want to be and how are you the most effective and how are you going to help a franchise win. So finding that out takes some time and takes some honest conversation."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=1595s

**7. Moe Wagner — "basketball is a simple game and I think the way that I approach every game, practice, every day" — Moe Wagner on the simplicity of basketball and the importance of doing your job** [27:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=1630s)

**Jordi Fernández:** "What's it like to make the transition to a new team, but knowing that there's expectations on you already?"

**Moe Wagner:** "I don't think there's no pressure. I mean, basketball is a simple game and I think the way that I approach every game, practice, every day is it just makes it that much easier because I mean to me it's just go out there and do your job and I mean if you go out there and do your job everything will take care of itself."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=1630s

**8. Moe Wagner — "I've always known that Brooklyn is something of a hard-working environment that everybody prides themselves on" — Moe Wagner on the importance of being a hard-working, diverse team** [28:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=1690s)

**Jordi Fernández:** "What did you know about Brooklyn before you signed here and what have you learned new now that you're here?"

**Moe Wagner:** "I mean, I will say that I've always known that Brooklyn is something of a hard-working environment that everybody prides themselves on, that's also very, very diverse. And yeah, those those things kind of, yeah, nothing changed after I signed here, just that I now go to work here. It's pretty awesome and I've really enjoyed that. My favorite player growing up was **Kevin Garnett** and to kind of I always think when I like shoot in the morning, I'm like, **Kevin Garnett** used to warm up here too, you know."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvj5tyv3dsE&t=1690s
