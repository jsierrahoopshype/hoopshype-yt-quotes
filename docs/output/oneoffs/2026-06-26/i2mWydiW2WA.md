**1. Brian Windhorst — "LeBron doesn't fix the guys not liking each other." — Channing Frye's comments on the Cavaliers' internal team issues** [00:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2mWydiW2WA&t=32s)

**Chris Oldach:** "A comment getting some run here in Cleveland was made by former Cav **Channing Frye** on the Road Trippin' podcast. The group was talking about where **LeBron James** is going to play next season, and **Channing Frye** said that the Cavs have soul issues. He said, quote, 'They got I don't like you issues and I don't want to be here with you issues,' and he said LeBron doesn't fix the guys not liking each other. What in the world is he talking about?"

**Brian Windhorst:** "I'm not aware of what he's talking about. I would say that when you watch the Knicks and the Cavs play in the conference finals, you definitely saw one team that showed way more drive and vision and energy and togetherness, but I didn't get the impression that it was because they didn't like each other. I got the impression they were outplayed, and the mental toughness aspect of the conference finals was definitely a factor. Questioning the Cavs' mental and physical toughness is fair to question, but I didn't get any togetherness issues."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2mWydiW2WA&t=32s

**2. Brian Windhorst — "It's unusual because as you can tell out there, the teams with their free agents are doing their business." — The Lakers' lack of communication with LeBron James during free agency** [03:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2mWydiW2WA&t=196s)

Brian Windhorst: "It's unusual because as you can tell out there, the teams with their free agents are doing their business. Not only getting guys resigned, but getting a good feel for where they are. The Lakers have two choices here. They have $50 million in cap space. They have to use that cap space to sign LeBron on some level if they're going to keep him. And so they either can prioritize LeBron or make him last. The way you prioritize him is by going to him first and getting his contract done first and then using the space you have left over. That's what you would normally do with a star player. Or they can go try to do their other business first and then try to offer LeBron what they have left. It appears like they're looking at option B, because specifically what they're doing right now is they're trying to trade for players into their salary cap space, trying to acquire a player with that $50 million in space and absorb them in. That's allowed in the NBA, and that seems to me right now to be their priority. And so they're prioritizing LeBron second."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2mWydiW2WA&t=196s

**5. Brian Windhorst — "You're going to have to explain to me how that trade would make sense if it's for Evan Mobley." — Possibility of a Jaylen Brown sign-and-trade with the Celtics** [07:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2mWydiW2WA&t=434s)

Brian Windhorst: "We're already seeing reports that a **Jaylen Brown** sign-and-trade with Boston could possibly be in the ether. You're going to have to explain to me how that trade would make sense if it's for **Evan Mobley**. I don't understand **Jaylen Brown**, Donovan Mitchell, and James Harden together. I don't see it. And beyond that, even if you got all of them to link arms and sing Kumbaya, you need size. Size is so important in the league today. I'm watching the draft and I'm watching Oklahoma City and San Antonio, two giant teams that got to the conference finals in the West, making moves to add size. Unless you're trading for an MVP-level player, I just cannot endorse trading front-line big men for non-bigs. I just don't think it's a prudent way to go forward, especially if you don't have another option."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2mWydiW2WA&t=434s
