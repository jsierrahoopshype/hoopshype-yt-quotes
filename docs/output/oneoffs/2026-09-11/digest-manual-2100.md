## Do Pro Athletes Deserve a Cut of Ownership? | PTFO — *PABLO TORRE FINDS OUT*

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4

_Speakers identified: Pablo Torre, David Samson, Domonique Foxworth_

**1. Domonique Foxworth — "I think players should be able to invest alongside in ownership groups and business opportunities." — Jaylen Brown on athletes investing in ownership groups** [03:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=200s)

**Domonique Foxworth:** "I think players should be able to invest alongside in ownership groups and business opportunities. I don't understand why that's ever been a thing, but it's like you're an athlete and they make it seem like they can control how much wealth or growth that you can actually accumulate. I think that's wrong. Also, I think that just like any other major corporation, if you work for Apple or if you work for Nike, if you're a CEO or if you're someone that's been on the board for a large amount of time, you get equity in the company at some point. You are a part of it, and I think athletes should be looked at in the same way."

**David Samson:** "See, technically, issues happen. It's the communication that's bothering me."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=200s

**2. David Samson — "League rules state that you cannot be a minority owner of a team. So if you want a piece of a team, then you have to stop playing." — David Samson on league rules regarding player minority ownership** [07:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=455s)

**David Samson:** "My answer was league rules state that you cannot be a minority owner of a team. So if you want a piece of a team, then you have to stop playing. And it turns out that that was never a trade that any player wanted to make because they realized that they'd make more money playing for the team than by giving us money to pay other players. But if you're asking whether I think players should be able to own parts of a team that they play on, I have no objection to that because it would be an arm's length transaction where the owners would have a valuation that a player would have to agree to as any limited partner investor has to agree to."

**Domonique Foxworth:** "I don't disagree with that. I guess the point I'm making is there's a difference between being awarded it as a part of your compensation package and what the NBA players have negotiated for is the opportunity to invest in private equity funds that then invest in the NBA."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=455s

**3. Domonique Foxworth — "Being granted shares as a part of your compensation, I'm all for that. That's fine." — Domonique Foxworth on the risks of player equity investments** [12:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=721s)

**Domonique Foxworth:** "Being granted shares as a part of your compensation, I'm all for that. That's fine. That's consistent with what people do in many businesses. The other thing is the thing that they actually have the ability to do, which I don't think benefits them at all. They have the ability to give their money to a private equity firm who will then invest in these teams at what the fair market value is. To me, that's not a benefit. These leagues are looking to increase franchise value and we keep finding that it's harder and harder for them to find pools of money big enough to buy their team. They'd be delighted if some of the money that they paid you in salary went back to supporting private equity firms that will then increase their franchise value."

**David Samson:** "It's very common, though, Domonique. That's how Wall Street, that's how finance works, where you get stock."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=721s

**4. David Samson — "If Jaylen Brown owned a share of the Celtics and is now an active player of the Sixers, I as a team operator would have an issue with that." — David Samson on the conflict of interest in player equity** [14:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=888s)

**David Samson:** "If **Jaylen Brown** owned a share of the Celtics and is now an active player of the Sixers, I as a team operator would have an issue with that because he would have access as a limited partner, depending on the agreement, he could have access to things about the Celtics that there would be a concern that would then be shared with the Sixers. There's just all sorts of conflicts that exist and there's ways to deal with it in the Wall Street world, fewer ways to deal with it in the sports world."

**Domonique Foxworth:** "Well, those seem like small hurdles that could be addressed. Those things we could invent ways to deal with it."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=888s

**5. David Samson — "The Koncaks are players who are so overpaid because teams have to spend money to get to the floor." — David Samson on the salary cap and overpaid players** [18:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=1080s)

David Samson: "The Koncaks are players who are so overpaid because teams have to spend money to get to the floor. And in a salary cap league, there's a floor. And NBA, because of that floor, you've got end-of-bench players right now making millions of dollars more than they were previously because the salary cap has gone up so much. And that's been a huge benefit to a large part of that union, Domonique, in my opinion."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v_n_vZtkPb4&t=1080s

## Richard ‘The Rizard’ Jefferson | Delly Love, Olympics Rage Bait & LeBron James GOAT Debate — *Triple M*

It felt like we were on a road trip together, and as a 46-year-old, you don't really get that very often anymore."

**2. Richard Jefferson — "There is no GOAT in basketball because there's multiple levels." — Richard Jefferson on why there is no single GOAT in basketball** [08:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ndwksu4hW8s&t=516s)

**James Brayshaw:** "Is **LeBron James** in your view the greatest player ever?"

**Richard Jefferson:** "There is no GOAT in basketball because there's multiple levels. **Kareem Abdul-Jabbar** I think had the greatest career, **Bill Russell** won the most championships, **Michael Jordan** is the icon, and I think **Kobe Bryant** and what he did mentally for the game and really for athletes all around the world. But I would just say if you were to build a basketball player, you could not build a better basketball player than **LeBron James**. There's no skill set he doesn't have, there's no physical ability. So for me, if I was building a basketball player, he would be the person that I would build."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ndwksu4hW8s&t=516s

**3. Richard Jefferson — "I love the grittiness to Australian players. It doesn't matter if you're an American player." — Richard Jefferson on the toughness of Australian NBA players** [13:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ndwksu4hW8s&t=828s)

Richard Jefferson: "I love the grittiness to Australian players. It doesn't matter if you're an American player, if you're a player that comes from Europe, if you're a player that comes from Australia, there's all a little bit of energy that each culture brings to the way they play basketball. With Australians, there's just a toughness to them, there's a grittiness to them. You don't find too many that will ever back down, and you love it. I have a tremendous amount of respect for anybody that will compete like their life is on the line, and you typically find that in Australian players."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ndwksu4hW8s&t=828s
