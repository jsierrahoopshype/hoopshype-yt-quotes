**1. Tracy McGrady — "It's that in-between. It's after shootaround which get people. You get up, you're tired, but then you go practice." — The physical toll of NBA road trips on young players** [00:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vyR3JC79fHo&t=5s)

**Tracy McGrady:** "I felt like it was a process. You take care of yourself and make sure you're ready to go. While doing that, you explain it, particularly that first road trip with rookies or guys who never done it. You explain it, sleep, that shootaround. We always do shootaround. Yeah, but it's different when you're doing shootaround on the road and particularly out West, the further left you go as an East Coast team. It's different because you try to get as much sleep as you need, but yet you still have a responsibility. Then in between, it's that in-between. It's after shootaround which get people. You get up, you're tired, but then you go practice. Now you're up. Now it's that between time you figure out, do I just stay up? Do I go for a walk? Do I go eat? What do I do? Because guys, I feel like you just have to, it's through trial and error."

**Vince Carter:** "A lot of these guys are coming from college where they didn't do it like that. They're the man, they're just ready to go. It's different when now you're the man, ready to go, you're in Minnesota and you got that boy **KG** like he said, or you're in LA, you got to deal with **Kobe**. We're in Houston and they got to go play you. You got to get your sleep. This preparation, so you find yourself, you're good for the first half until that third quarter hit."

Tracy McGrady: "It bothered me seeing young guys getting on the bus that second, now they have three buses. Young guys getting on the second and third bus.
