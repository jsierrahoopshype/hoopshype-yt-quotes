"Just the composure, the composure that only a champion knows something about. They went there, think about it, they were down 15 to nothing to start the game and they ended the game winning by 15. So from the point of being down 15 nothing, they outscored **San Antonio** by 30. They held them to 31% shooting from three-point range. As effective as **Shai Gilgeous-Alexander** was, the reigning league MVP, two-time MVP, with 26 and 12 on the night, **Alex Caruso** and **Jared McCain** were both a plus-28 on the basketball court. McCain comes off the bench and gives you 24 points. The other **Jalen Williams**, while J-Dub was on the bench hurt with his hamstring, the other **Jalen Williams** that was going to come in there and was supposed to be a big body to spell for **Isaiah Hartenstein** or **Chet Holmgren**, still manages to chip in about 17 points. Everywhere you turn, no matter what it is, they have something to answer whatever challenge that you throw in their direction. And that's what they did tonight going into **San Antonio** for Game 3 to reclaim home court advantage in this series."

**2. Mark Daigneault — "He is like the ultimate compete together player. His intangibles are through the roof." — Jalen Williams' intangibles and competitive spirit define team identity** [19:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KnA_wz8EnvI&t=1154s)

"We talk about our team identity being like being a compete together team. We want to be a team that, even if you don't know anything about basketball and you're watching us play, we want you to say this team competes together. And he is like the ultimate compete together player. His intangibles are through the roof. He's an unbelievable team guy. He's always inside the team. He is really a glue inside of our locker room. Even a guy like **McCain**, **Jalen Williams** is one of the first guys always that pulls guys into the team and into the club. But there's one thing to be a team guy, he also, he's a badass competitor, and he leans into the competition, he's not afraid. And he's got weaknesses as a player, but his intangibles just swallow those up. And so it just gives you unbelievable confidence to put him in any situation and really whatever situation we put him in, he's ready to rock."

**3. Iman Shumpert — "They play within a team offense. One guy drives, he penetrates, he kicks it out." — Oklahoma City's well-oiled machine offense is tough to game plan for** [12:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KnA_wz8EnvI&t=728s)

"It becomes tough. What's tough to consider, I look more at the plus-minus from **Alex Caruso** tonight because the amount of extra possessions he provided OKC with was enough to be another 20 points if it was just given to one player. **Jared McCain** did a great job of just continuing to be aggressive. They play within a team offense. One guy drives, he penetrates, he kicks it out. They change sides of the floor. They hit the corner man and always have somebody going to the corner. If they have a double team, they fill the middle. It's just a well-oiled machine, next guy up type of system."

**4. Stephen A. Smith — "Isaiah Hartenstein has been doing to him the last two games and it's been somewhat effective." — Isaiah Hartenstein's physicality effectively derails Wembanyama's offensive production.** [05:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KnA_wz8EnvI&t=346s)

"You got **Isaiah Hartenstein** out there bodying him up at every turn, putting a body on him. **Wembanyama** danced on him at the beginning of the game and pulled up from three, no doubt about that. But also, the physicality that Oklahoma City was throwing in **Wembanyama**'s direction sort of derailed him to some degree. He's still great, we all know that. But the thing about it is at 7'5" to be as slim as he is, I don't give a damn who you are, whatever the opposing team is, if you have somebody remotely in the same size stratosphere as him, what you're going to do is try to use physicality to your advantage because you know he's real thin. And that's what you tried to do, and that's what **Isaiah Hartenstein** has been doing to him the last two games and it's been somewhat effective. He still got some numbers, but they weren't 41 and 24 like they were in Game 1."

**5. Stephen A. Smith — "for once, you didn't have Stephon Castle, as good as he is." — Vassell, De'Aaron Fox, and Stephon Castle's improved play.** [05:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KnA_wz8EnvI&t=346s)

**6. Mark Daigneault — "You just got to get it back on track, but you can't like wait for it to turn, you got to make it turn." — Thunder's coaching strategy to overcome early 15-0 deficit** [21:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KnA_wz8EnvI&t=1265s)

"It's a 48-minute game. Reminding them that half of those points are controllable, like game plan-oriented things. In a weird way, I think that gives the team confidence when it feels more controllable because it gives you a sense of agency over what happens in the next 46 minutes or the next 45 minutes or whatever it was. But this team, we've been in these situations before. We understand it's a 48-minute game and you just got to get it back on track, but you can't like wait for it to turn, you got to make it turn. And I thought we did a great job of that."

**11. Iman Shumpert — "The mid-range game is something that people don't understand, but it can slow everything down for not only the player, for the team." — Wembanyama needs to develop a mid-range game for offensive consistency** [13:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KnA_wz8EnvI&t=792s)

"I don't know. At times I think that they could use him more, but I consider what they've been doing all year. They haven't been forcing the issue. They're not a forceful team. They're going to take what the defense gives to them. And right now, with what OKC is doing defensively with him, making sure to keep a body on him, making sure to take away that freedom to run, that freedom to jump, the freedom to be athletic, and force him to rely on his skill work. I think the only thing that **San Antonio** could do to help improve anything for **Victor Wembanyama** is to make sure that he can get to his mid-range. The mid-range game is something that people don't understand, but it can slow everything down for not only the player, for the team, to let them understand that when we need a big bucket, I can get the shot that we need, even if it got to kiss it off the glass, but a controlled 15 to 18-footer is what he needs to go to."
