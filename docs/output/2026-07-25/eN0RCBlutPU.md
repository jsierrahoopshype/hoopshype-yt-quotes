**1. Chris Mannix — "he is now doing in Portland. He got to Carolina and he shook up the front office." — Tom Dunden is applying a Carolina Hurricanes-style playbook to the Portland Trail Blazers** [03:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eN0RCBlutPU&t=185s)

Chris Mannix: "I think he is running the Carolina Hurricanes playbook down to the last detail. Everything that he did in Carolina when he took over, he is now doing in Portland. He got to Carolina and he shook up the front office. The head coach was gone within a matter of months, the general manager was gone. Efficiency is the term for some of the cost-cutting, but he gutted some of the personnel in that office. He also had a similar battle with the city of Raleigh over some of the money for the stadium that he is having right now with the city of Portland. But he has taken the Carolina Hurricanes playbook and he has dropped it into Portland and on the Trail Blazers."

**2. Chris Mannix — "one of the things that makes teams successful, small-market especially teams successful, is the investment they make" — Small-market NBA teams must prioritize investing in front-office brain power** [05:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eN0RCBlutPU&t=303s)

Chris Mannix: "Does the model that was successful in hockey work in basketball? Because even if you pour money into the team and are willing to go into the tax aprons, one of the things that makes teams successful, small-market especially teams successful, is the investment they make in brain power. The money they spend on front-office work, on scouting, all the things that make some of these small-market teams great. Oklahoma City is great because of **Sam Presti** at the top, but they also have a deep staff of basketball operations, a detailed scouting department. They invest a lot of money in personnel."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eN0RCBlutPU&t=303s

Chris Mannix: "Look at San Antonio. **Sean Elliott** last year with the Spurs, how valuable was he? They paid a lot of money to get him out of Dallas. **6. Chris Mannix — "building an NBA team, successful one, has never been harder. It's never been harder because even if" — Building a successful NBA team is harder than ever before** [11:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eN0RCBlutPU&t=687s)

Chris Mannix: "Building an NBA team, successful one, has never been harder. It's never been harder because even if you find the magical elixir, within a year or two you have to break it up because these guys get too expensive. Look at what Boston has done, look at what Oklahoma City is now doing. Eventually San Antonio is going to get there. Everybody has to shake up their roster because of these second aprons. You have to have a full and robust staff in your front office."
