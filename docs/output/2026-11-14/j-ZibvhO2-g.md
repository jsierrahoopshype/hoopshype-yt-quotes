# Perk Wants Nick Nurse to Rethink LeBron’s Role in Philadelphia — *Road Trippin*

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-ZibvhO2-g

_Speakers identified: Allie Clifton, Kendrick Perkins, Richard Jefferson_

**1. Kendrick Perkins — "the only way that Philadelphia could look better offensively, in my humble opinion, is moving LeBron to the center position" — Kendrick Perkins suggests moving LeBron James to the center position** [00:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-ZibvhO2-g&t=15s)

**Kendrick Perkins:** "The only way that Philadelphia could look better offensively, in my humble opinion, is moving LeBron to the center position. I think it would actually benefit LeBron even more. Imagine him having to slide his puppies to guard a slower person on the perimeter because most bigs are hanging on the perimeter. I would rather guard LeBron have to deal with an Embiid or **Jared Allen** than have to deal with the speed of a **Jason Tatum** or one of those quick perimeter players."

**Allie Clifton:** "He is still averaging 33 minutes a game though. Make that make sense of putting him in the center spot."

**Richard Jefferson:** "Well, the center spot because a lot of centers now are stretch centers and they are not back to the basket players. Now again, you do not necessarily want him guarding the Jokics and the Rudy Goberts for long extended periods. But could he guard against, maybe not Jalen Duren."

**Kendrick Perkins:** "He could guard Kyle Anthony Towns. He could guard Evan Mobley."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-ZibvhO2-g&t=15s

**2. Richard Jefferson — "they do not need a feared LeBron. A feared LeBron is an individual that when you get into a playoff series, he can mentally figure out where to get the guys" — Richard Jefferson argues LeBron James provides value as a cerebral leader** [01:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-ZibvhO2-g&t=111s)

Richard Jefferson: "They do not need a feared LeBron. A feared LeBron is an individual that when you get into a playoff series, he can mentally figure out where to get the guys, where the shots are coming, how to get it done. That is where he is at his most fearful is at his brain and what he is capable of doing, the manipulation of an offense in a series. That is the ultimate value. That is the experience that he brings. He knows all the plays, knows the rotations. So you get into a playoff series and it is really a chess match. I want that player on my chess team."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-ZibvhO2-g&t=111s

**3. Kendrick Perkins — "I would like Nick Nurse, at least for the first, second and third quarter, to stagger him and Joel Embiid minutes" — Kendrick Perkins wants Nick Nurse to stagger LeBron James and Joel Embiid** [03:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-ZibvhO2-g&t=210s)

Kendrick Perkins: "I would like **Nick Nurse**, at least for the first, second and third quarter, to stagger him and **Joel Embiid** minutes. I think the Philadelphia 76ers benefit when they are able to play small and play with pace. If not, then all of a sudden you have **Joel Embiid** who is struggling tremendously offensively for his shooting percentage because he is being asked to space the floor. But he really wants a heavy dose to be able to go onto the low posts and it is part of sacrifice."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-ZibvhO2-g&t=210s

**4. Richard Jefferson — "they did not just add LeBron James. They added Jaylen Brown. So they added a supercharged version of Paul George" — Richard Jefferson on the sacrifice required for Philadelphia's new roster** [04:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j-ZibvhO2-g&t=243s)

Richard Jefferson: "That is a sacrifice. You do not have 25 shots to get your rhythm in for a former MVP, a guy that has been top in the MVP race. They did not just add **LeBron James**. They added **Jaylen Brown**. So they added a supercharged version of **Paul George** who is hungry and going to go get it. So you add that, so that should lower his usage rate. You add **LeBron James**, that lowers your usage rate. So now just your overall rhythm and feel for the first time, maybe in your career, is completely different."
