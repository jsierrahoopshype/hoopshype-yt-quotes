## Perk Called Playoff Experience OVERRATED — RJ Wasn’t Buying It — *Road Trippin*

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE

_Speakers identified: Kendrick Perkins, Richard Jefferson, Allie Clifton, Channing Frye_

**1. Richard Jefferson — "If you haven't played a hundred and something games over the course of your career, then those last 30 games are the most excruciating." — Richard Jefferson on the importance of playoff experience for NBA teams** [00:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=8s)

**Kendrick Perkins:** "When you say lack of experience, what do you mean? Lack of experience offensively, assignments, making adjustments?"

**Richard Jefferson:** "If you haven't played a hundred and something games over the course of your career, then those last 30 games are the most excruciating, emotionally, physically. If you haven't played a team four or five times in a row, 90% of their roster hasn't. That's experience. Playoff experience means that you've gone through the ups and downs. It doesn't mean that your offense isn't in game, your game isn't nice, you don't have the best players, you don't know the systems. But if you've never done anything along that journey that starts in mid-April and goes to the end of June, that is two months of basketball that is very different basketball."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=8s

**2. Kendrick Perkins — "the whole playoff experience shit is overrated to me." — Kendrick Perkins argues that playoff experience is overrated in the NBA** [01:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=112s)

**Kendrick Perkins:** "I also know that the whole playoff experience shit is overrated to me. Really, when it comes to the postseason, it comes down to talent is one, luck is another, but it comes down to who are the guys in the locker room and what type of basketball IQ they have."

**Richard Jefferson:** "Give me an example."

**Kendrick Perkins:** "Every game in the series is different, and all you have to do is be locked in. If you're an immature group, experience may kick in, but I'm not getting those vibes from this group right here."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=112s

**3. Richard Jefferson — "your top seven players are playoff experience proven people. I'm saying it hasn't been done." — Richard Jefferson on the lack of playoff experience for the current San Antonio Spurs** [04:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=276s)

**Richard Jefferson:** "Five or six teams have gone to the playoffs and then went on to go to the NBA Finals or win a championship after not going to the playoffs the year before. You want me to give you some examples? **Kevin Garnett** had had multiple playoff appearances. He's been to the conference finals. **Paul Pierce**, I played against him in the conference finals, and he had multiple playoff appearances. So your two best players are playoff experience proven people. I'm saying it hasn't been done."

**Kendrick Perkins:** "I'm talking about me. I'm talking about you also had two guys on your team that went to Eastern Conference Finals."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=276s

**4. Channing Frye — "Can you sacrifice for your team? He's never been doing that." — Playoff experience requires players to sacrifice and adapt their roles** [07:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=440s)

Channing Frye: "Playoff experience is like having vets. When I went to Phoenix, they had already had playoff experience and they were like, 'Hey Channing, throughout the year, we're going to need you to work on these things that are uncomfortable so that people don't have counters to you throughout the playoffs so that we can have offensive versatility.' So that's one thing that they have never seen. A lot of teams are not going to put out their best defense versus the Spurs. They're going to try something maybe once or twice, but then they're going to go back on film and they're going to work on that secretly if they need to play the Spurs. The second thing is, can some of these guys sacrifice? They may say, 'You know what, **Devin Vassell**, we're going to double team you and you're not going to be able to score the ball so you're going to become one-dimensional.' Can you sacrifice for your team? He's never been doing that. Doing that for one game is different than getting criticized or hearing the noise outside of those playoff situations. And then can you keep your head for guys coming off the bench? Let's say **Luke Kornet**, he's obviously a champion, but you go, 'Hey Luke, we can't play you this series.' Is he going to be able to still work out, be professional? Think about some of the other young guys. Can they continue to be available even though coach says, 'Hey, this may not be the matchup for you.' Can you do that?"

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=440s

**5. Kendrick Perkins — "Can you make adjustments on the fly? Let's say the team, they start triple teaming Wembanyama." — Victor Wembanyama must learn to make adjustments against defensive pressure** [07:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=440s)

Kendrick Perkins: "And then can you make adjustments on the fly? Let's say the team, they start triple teaming **Wembanyama**. I don't know, let's say they do that. Is he going to be able to pass the paint, make the right three?"

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl73WZoqKzE&t=440s

## Lou Williams Opens Up on His Sixth Man Legacy, NBA Journey & What Happened in the Bubble — *Cousins with Vince Carter & Tracy McGrady*

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ

_Speakers identified: Vince Carter, Tracy McGrady, Lou Williams_

**1. Lou Williams — "I've lost more Sixth Man than I've won. I've came in second four times." — Lou Williams on his runner-up finishes for Sixth Man of the Year** [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=0s)

Lou Williams: "I don't think people realize that I've lost more Sixth Man than I've won. I've came in second four times. I've been a runner-up four times. I've lost to **James Harden**, I've lost to **JR Smith**, and then I lost to **Eric Gordon**, and I lost to Trez, my teammate. I've lost twice to two of my teammates."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=0s

**2. Lou Williams — "It was the misconception that I snuck out of the bubble. That was the funny part." — Lou Williams on the misconception about leaving the NBA bubble** [03:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=186s)

Lou Williams: "Looking back at COVID and those times, I think everybody in the bubble would have did what I did if you had the opportunity to leave. It was the misconception that I snuck out of the bubble. That was the funny part that came out of it. I didn't get fined, I didn't get suspended or nothing. It was just, at that time, COVID, everything that was out of the bubble, everybody was sensitive about it."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=186s

**3. Lou Williams — "I'm originally from Memphis, Tennessee. A lot of people don't really know that." — Lou Williams on his upbringing in Memphis and basketball journey** [06:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=367s)

Lou Williams: "I'm originally from Memphis, Tennessee. A lot of people don't really know that. I say I'm from Atlanta, but I'm from Memphis, Tennessee. I was an outside kid, I was an inner-city kid, I was dabbling, dabbling, just being a little adventurous. Some things was happening in my life early on. My father had passed away when I was eight, my brother had got sentenced to some substantial jail time around that same year in my life, and so around nine, my mom was like, 'We up out of here,' ended up moving me to Atlanta. So I landed in Atlanta when I was nine years old."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=367s

**4. Lou Williams — "Sebastian Telfair, 2003. We were at USA, out in Colorado, training for Junior Olympics." — Lou Williams on realizing he was a professional prospect** [17:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=1051s)

Lou Williams: "**Sebastian Telfair**, 2003. We were at USA, out in Colorado, training for Junior Olympics or World Games, and we were watching the draft together. At 13, **Sebastian Telfair** went, and that was the first time I thought to myself, 'Oh shit, I can do this.' At that time, it was guys like you, Vince and Tracy, already physically there. You're 6'7, 6'8, 6'9, y'all are built like men. That was the first time I saw a guy my size have that opportunity, and I'm like, 'Man, if the door is open for guys at this size, and I think I am who I am, I'm out of here. I'm going.'"

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=1051s

**5. Lou Williams — "I didn't want to waste no time with college. And so, it never was appeasing to me." — Lou Williams on his decision to skip college for the NBA** [21:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=1273s)

Lou Williams: "I didn't want to waste no time with college. And so, it never was appeasing to me. I wanted to know my visits, I didn't care about the girls, I knew the league had better girls, I went tripping on that. I didn't care about the parties, I knew the league party better, I knew all of that. And so, I just got to a point I was anti-college. I was anti-school."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=1273s

**6. Lou Williams — "The most challenging thing for me my rookie year was just to be able to find a role." — Lou Williams on the challenges of his rookie season** [27:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=1643s)

Lou Williams: "The most challenging thing for me my rookie year was just to be able to find a role, be able to find my place in being an 18-year-old kid, not knowing you're going to play, but knowing you're expected to show up, knowing you're expected to do all of the extra things. How do you stay motivated and how do I stick if I'm not going to play being so young? And having a coach in Mo Cheeks that thought I was a pass-first guard. The way that I played, the way that you can get the most out of me, I wasn't really getting trained to be who I was naturally."

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=1643s

**7. Lou Williams — "I feel like I got robbed twice. My last year with the Sixers, I thought I let that team in scoring." — Lou Williams on his Sixth Man of the Year awards** [33:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=2016s)

Lou Williams: "I feel like I got robbed twice. My last year with the Sixers, I thought I let that team in scoring. With the Clippers, that I was averaging about 22, 23 a game or something like that, off the bench, and I didn't make it. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdcVejAMaNQ&t=2016s

What's happened has happened, but if you're asking me in retrospect, what the cause of us not getting there was, the week that we lost, we had a bunch of distractions from a lot of different places."
